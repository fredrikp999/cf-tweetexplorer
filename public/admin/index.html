Find tweets

Search X for any topic and see what the tweets add up to.

How this works and what it costs

Find tweets matching a query and see what they add up to. Good for a quick look at a topic right now or over the last few days.

  • Type words or a phrase, then narrow things down with the options: sort order, how many tweets, when they were posted, minimum likes, originals only, media or links, language and a specific account.
  • The Searching for line shows the exact query those options build (X's advanced search operators), so you can also type operators such as from:, min_faves: or since_time: yourself.
  • From lists limits the search to the accounts in one or more of your lists (made under Lists and templates). It combines with every other option; a long list is split into several searches automatically.
  • You get stats, charts and the tweet list. Click a chart bar to filter the list, click a tweet's text to open its replies, conversation, quotes and retweeters.

Cost: about $0.15 per 1,000 tweets. For long time ranges use Archives; to be alerted use Live feed.

How this works and what it costs

Look at one account: profile card (bio, followers, location) plus an analysis of their most recent tweets.

  • Shows when they post, what they tag, which tweets did best, and how often they reply.
  • Pages of recent tweets: 1–5 (about 20 each). For anything older than that, use Archives with from:handle.
  • Clicking an author's name anywhere in the app opens them here.

Cost: one profile lookup plus about $0.15 per 1,000 tweets.

Lists and templates

Everything you maintain in one place: account lists, feed lists, X lists, and the audio and image templates used by briefings.

How this works and what it costs

A list is a named set of accounts you care about for one topic, for example "AI labs" or "Security researchers".

  • Paste handles or profile links (many at once). Each account is checked and shown with its picture and follower count. Accounts that don't exist are flagged.
  • Load recent tweets pulls the latest posts from every account in the list and analyses them like a search. It also shows which accounts have been quiet.
  • Watch in Live feed turns the list into live watches, so new tweets from any member show up in the Live feed.
  • Use a list as a filter in Find tweets, Archives and Live feed: pick it and combine it with any other option, such as minimum likes, language or a date range.
  • Lists are saved. Add a note to any account to remember why it is there.

Cost: about $0.0002 to check an account, then about $0.15 per 1,000 tweets loaded. Watching costs the same as any Monitor watch; a long list is split into a few watches.

How feed lists work

A feed list is a named set of sources you follow for a topic: YouTube channels and playlists, RSS / Atom feeds and Hacker News searches, in any mix.

  • Paste links, one per line. A channel (@Fireship or a link), a playlist, or a feed address: each is checked by loading it once, and named after the feed.
  • Summarize latest reads every feed in the list now and summarizes what it published in the period. Briefings can use a feed list as a source in the same way.
  • Watch in Live feed adds a watch per feed, so new items appear in the live feed.

Cost: nothing for the feeds. Summaries cost the language model only.

Lists that live on x.com, kept by reference: nothing is copied. Signal

Everything you watch, combined into one read of what is being talked about, and where.

How the score works
  • Every watch belongs to a group: the feed list it was made from, or its kind of source (X, RSS, Hacker News, YouTube), or a name you give it. Groups vote.
  • A word, two-word phrase or hashtag is scored by the share of a group's items that mention it, averaged over the groups, each counted by its weight (0 mutes a group, 3 triples its voice). It is shares, not counts, so a busy group cannot drown a small one.
  • Everywhere means most groups mention it. Only in … means nearly all of it comes from one group. Rising compares the window with the one before it.
  • Topics are counted as items are captured (by counting words, or named by a language model if you turned that on under Signal setup) and kept for 30 days. Only what your watches capture is counted. What feeds Signal, how groups are weighted and what is published are set under Signal setup; this tab only shows the result.

Top-level Signals

A Signal is a named set of groups. Publish one to give it its own page on the public site, at /signal/<address>. The public page shows group names and topics; turn on Show sources to let each topic open the items behind it, with their sources and links to the originals.

Sources

What is watched: X accounts and searches, RSS feeds, YouTube and Hacker News. New items appear in the Live feed and feed Signal.

How this works and what it costs

Get new tweets as they are posted, from an account or a search query, without keeping the page open.

  • The server checks each watch every N seconds (15 s to 1 h) using a since_time window, and pushes anything new to this page live.
  • Watches are saved: restart the server and nothing is missed or repeated (tweets are de-duplicated by ID).
  • A tab badge and optional desktop notifications tell you when something arrives while you are elsewhere.
  • RSS / Atom feeds, YouTube channels and playlists (new videos, from their public feeds) and Hacker News (the front page, new stories, Show HN, Ask HN, or a keyword with a points threshold) are watched the same way and are free. The live feed shows every source combined, or one source at a time.

Cost (X): this is polling, so you pay for checks even when nothing is posted. 1 minute ≈ $6.50/month per watch while idle; 5 minutes ≈ $1.30; 15 minutes ≈ $0.43. A daily cap (default $2) pauses the X watches when reached; RSS and Hacker News carry on.

Archives

Collect and analyse tweets across weeks, months or years.

How this works and what it costs

Collect a large archive of tweets over a date range, then analyse it. Use this for "everything about X in 2025" instead of the latest page of results.

  • Splits the range into time shards and slides until_time backwards inside each (no cursors, which loop on old data). Duplicates are removed.
  • Probe & estimate samples one day (under a cent) to estimate volume and suggest a shard size. Collection stops at your tweet cap (approximate).
  • Jobs run on the server, can be stopped and resumed, survive restarts, and save raw JSONL you can download.
  • Analysis adds a weekday × hour heatmap, engagement over time, languages and most-used words. Lists show the first 200 tweets; click a chart bar to narrow down.

Cost: about $0.15 per 1,000 tweets, and you confirm the maximum before starting.

Costs

What this app has spent on X data, text models, audio (speech) models and image models.

How this is counted
  • X data is counted at twitterapi.io's price ($0.15 per 1,000 tweets, $0.18 per 1,000 profiles, at least one tweet's worth per call) from the size of each answer.
  • Text models (the ones that write summaries and briefings) are counted at the model's list price times the tokens used. Models without a price here count as zero.
  • Audio models (Gemini TTS on Replicate, for audio versions and template samples) are counted separately, from the token counts Replicate reports for each clip at a price calibrated on Replicate's own figures. They are not part of the text-model figures.
  • Image models (Replicate, for the pictures on briefings and template samples) are counted separately at a flat price per picture that depends on the model (Replicate's prices, see IMAGE_MODELS in the code).
  • Everything that spends goes into one ledger: Search, Users and Lists, History, Monitor, the Summarize tab, and scheduled briefings. A failed briefing still shows what its data cost.
  • These are estimates. Your real balance is the credits count at the bottom of the sidebar. Costs are shown only in this private area, never on the public pages.

Ask AI

Have AI read a source and write you the gist.

How this works and what it costs

Pick what to summarize and the agent fetches the posts or items itself with read-only tools (from X, feeds, YouTube or Hacker News), then writes a summary with links back to what it used.

  • Sources: the latest from one of your lists, a search, what the Live feed has captured, the tweets already on your screen (use Summarize these tweets under Find tweets or list results), or just describe what you want.
  • It can look up replies or more tweets when it needs context. Everything it does is shown under What the agent did.
  • The agent is built with the Strands Agents SDK, and the same code runs here and on Cloudflare. Each run is capped (turns, tokens and tweets) so it can't run away, and it stops if you click Stop or leave the page.
  • Pick the AI model under Model; each shows its context size, price and what testing showed. The tweets it reads go to the model provider (OpenRouter, or Anthropic if you configure that).

Cost: the usual twitterapi.io price for any X tweets it fetches (about $0.15 per 1,000; feeds, YouTube and Hacker News are free), plus the model's usage. A typical list summary is about 6,000 tokens in and 1,000 out, roughly $0.002 with the default model; the actual tokens and cost are shown after each run.

Ask AI

Chat with Claude Code. It fetches the data and explains it.

How this works and what it costs

Ask questions in plain language instead of picking endpoints. Claude Code runs as an agent over the Agent Client Protocol (ACP) with the twitterapi-io skill and calls the API itself.

  • Good for open-ended or multi-step questions: "compare these two accounts", "what changed after the launch?".
  • It shows each tool it uses. Every shell command needs your approval in a card; read it and choose Allow or Reject.
  • When numbers suit a chart, it can return one, and this page draws it.
  • It is told to use read endpoints only. It has no login cookies, so it cannot post.

Cost: twitterapi.io credits for what it fetches, plus your Claude usage for the conversation.

Post as X

Optional. Log in to like, retweet, reply, follow and post as your X account.

How this works and what it costs

Optional. Log in as an X account to act from this app. Everything else here only reads public data.

  • Once logged in, every tweet shows Reply, Quote, Like, Retweet, Bookmark, Follow, DM and Delete buttons, and you can compose a tweet or load your bookmarks.
  • Login needs a residential proxy and costs 10,000 credits (about $0.10); each action costs 60 credits. Retweet, follow, delete and posting ask you to confirm.
  • Your password is sent once to twitterapi.io and not stored. The session cookies stay on the server in .data/session.json and never reach the browser.

Posting and following are public and affect a real account, so go slowly: rapid automated actions can get an account restricted.

Reading a tweet: drill-down

Click the text of any tweet to open a panel with its replies (sortable; expand any reply for its own replies), the conversation around it, quotes and retweeters. Click an author's name to open their profile in User. Esc closes the panel; Back steps through tweets you opened.

Reading the charts

On Find tweets, Look up account and Archives, click a bar in a chart (time bucket, hour, hashtag, author, language, word) to filter the tweet list underneath; show all clears it. Times are in your local time zone.

What things cost
  • Tweets: about $0.15 per 1,000 ($0.00015 each). An empty call is billed as one tweet.
  • Profiles: about $0.18 per 1,000.
  • Write actions: 60 credits ($0.0006); login 10,000 credits ($0.10).
  • The Costs tab shows what has actually been spent, per day, per activity and per briefing. Briefings shows each report's cost, Briefing setup an estimate while you set up a schedule, and Budgets every spending limit in one place.
  • Your remaining credits are shown at the bottom of the sidebar. Monitor has a daily spend cap; History asks before it starts.
Rate limits

Free accounts allow about one request every 5 seconds, so multi-page loads are slow; the app waits and retries automatically. Paid accounts allow far more, and History can then run up to 12 shards in parallel.

Where your data lives
  • Your twitterapi.io key is a Cloudflare secret. It is never sent to the browser.
  • Watches, captured tweets and History collections are stored in this site's own Cloudflare storage and run on Cloudflare timers, so they keep working while your browser is closed.
  • Access is limited to the people your Cloudflare Access policy allows.
  • This online version is read-only. The Agent tab and the Account tab (posting, liking, following) only exist when you run the app on your own computer.
Where your data lives
  • .env: your API key. It stays on the server and is never sent to the browser.
  • .data/history/: collected tweets (JSONL) and job progress.
  • .data/monitor/: watches, checkpoints and the captured-tweet feed.
  • .data/session.json: X login cookies, only if you log in on the Account tab.
  • Everything is local; this app listens on 127.0.0.1 only and refuses requests from other websites.
Search operators worth knowing
  • from:name tweets by an account; to:name replies to one
  • "exact phrase", -word to exclude, a OR b
  • min_faves:100, min_retweets:20, lang:en
  • -filter:replies originals only; filter:media with media
  • since_time: / until_time: Unix seconds for date windows (History sets these for you)
